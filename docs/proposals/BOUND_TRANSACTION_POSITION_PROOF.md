## 1. Definitions

Every name below is the code's, given a symbol for the proof.

- A **pattern** P is a data pattern `[e a v]`, `[e a v t]` or `[e a v t added]`. Its positions are E, A, V, T and the fifth. A position is **bound** when P holds a constant there or a variable the binding or the query's inputs bind, and **free** otherwise.
- A **view** is the matcher's mode and target. The **latest** view holds every datom in the store. The view **as of** τ holds every datom whose Tx is at or below τ, which `shouldFilterTx` decides. **History** holds every datom and resolves nothing. The latest and as-of views are the **resolving views**.
- A datom's **Tx** is the ElementID of the operation that wrote it. A bound T names an ElementID, spelled `#eid [lamport replica]` in the text. The T position is inhabited by ElementID alone, as E is by Identity and A by Keyword: a constant or an input of another type is refused at the user boundary (`query.ValidateTransactionBinding`, at the match's entry and at the query's entry), and a value of another type joined into the position from elsewhere is the equality join's typed non-match, selected out at the match's entry (`typedPositionBindingCheck`).
- An **(E, A) group** is the set of datoms of one entity and one attribute in the view.
- The **resolved datoms** of a group in a resolving view are what the cardinality the attribute resolves under makes of the group:
  - Cardinality one under the plain rule: the group's datom with the highest Tx when it asserts, and none when it withdraws or the group is empty.
  - Cardinality one under the unique rule: the walk's emission, the group's highest-Tx assertion that no later withdrawal of its value cancels and no other entity's later claim of its value supersedes (SCHEMA, "Read-time resolution"), and none when no assertion survives.
  - Cardinality many: per value, the group's highest-Tx operation carrying it, when it asserts; an assertion at a withdrawal's Lamport counts as later (`processAddWins`).
  - Cardinality vector: one datom whose V is the collection the RGA rule reconstructs and whose Tx is the highest ElementID among the group's operations (`resolveRGAGroup`).
- In History every datom is its own resolved datom.
- A pattern **admits** a datom when `matchesDatom` holds: the datom's value at each bound position equals the bound value, E by identity, A by keyword, V by `datalog.ValuesEqual`, Tx by ElementID, and the fifth by whether the datom asserts.
- The **answer** of P in a view is the set of resolved datoms of the view that P admits, each projected to P's variables. This is the contract DATOMIC_COMPATIBILITY states for the fifth position, "in the latest view and an as-of view every tuple is a resolved value", read for every position: a tuple of a resolving view's answer is a resolved datom's.
- A **run** is a `ScanBound`, an index with the leading components bound to values, read in index order.
- A run **holds a group whole** when every datom of the group lies in the run, adjacent to the group's other datoms (SEEK §1).
- A run is **narrowed to V** when the bound value is among its prefix components, AVET `[A][V]` or VAET `[V]`. A run is **narrowed to T** when the bound ElementID is among them, TAEV `[T]` or ATEV `[A][T]`.
- A **candidate** is a datom the resolving iterator emits from a run that does not hold the datom's group whole.
- A resolution's **version** is the highest ElementID among the datoms the resolution read. The resolved datom's **Tx** is its own.

## 2. Preconditions

- **P1** (`matchesDatom`). The function compares a datom's E, A, V, Tx and operation against the values bound at the pattern's positions, a nil standing for a free position, and the Tx by value against an ElementID, given as a value or through a pointer; a bound Tx of another type is unreachable past the boundary and the entry selection, and the function asserts on one.
- **P2** (the unbound route). `matchUnboundScan` reads P's constants at E, A, V and T, picks a run through `chooseIndex(e, a, v, tx)`, wraps the scan in `CRDTResolvingIterator` in a resolving view and leaves it raw in History, and `unboundIterator.Next` admits each datom the iterator emits through `matchesDatom(datom, e, a, v, tx, added)`.
- **P3** (`chooseIndex` with E free and T bound). A bound A with V free gives ATEV under `[A][T]`. A free A with V free gives TAEV under `[T]`. A bound A with V bound gives AVET under `[A][V]`, and a free A with V bound gives VAET under `[V]`, the V arm preceding the T arm. A bound E gives an EATV or EAVT run under E whatever T holds.
- **P4** (INDEX Theorem 1a). A run on EATV, EAVT, AETV or AEVT whose prefix ends at or before the attribute holds every group it meets whole, in Tx-descending order within the cardinality-one group.
- **P5** (the resolving iterator's emission). For a cardinality-one group under the plain rule, `CRDTResolvingIterator.Next` emits the group's first datom when it asserts and nothing when it withdraws; under the unique rule it emits the first datom `walkApplyEntry` answers Emit for. For a many group it emits each value's first assertion not withdrawn at a later Lamport. For a vector group it emits one datom, the collection and the group's highest ElementID.
- **P6** (a run narrowed to T). Such a run holds the datoms of one ElementID. A cardinality-one group has at most one datom per ElementID (INDEX Theorem 1b, "each (E, A) can appear at most once per transaction"), so the iterator emits every assertion in the run as its group's first datom.
- **P7** (the V-constant route). `matchUnboundScan` sends a pattern with E free, V constant, A free or resolving as cardinality one, outside History, to `matchWithVValidation`, under a condition that reads `e`, `v`, `a`, the cardinality and the mode and not `tx`. `validatingVBoundIterator` holds no field for P's T, `Next` admits a cardinality-one candidate when `validateCandidate` answers that the pair's current value is the bound value, passes a many candidate through, and skips a vector candidate; `tryEmitUniqueWinner` builds the claimant's datom from `resolveAVLWW`'s identity and Tx and emits it without reading T. `query.PatternExtractor.ExtractT` is called nowhere on the route.
- **P8** (the binding route). `analyzeReuseStrategy` counts the positions whose variable the binding binds and no constant. With V the one such position it requests validation for a constant attribute resolving as cardinality one and for a variable attribute, outside History, and `MatchWithConstraints` runs `matchWithVValidation` at or below `executor.SmallBindingBoundary` and `matchWithHashJoin` on AETV above it under a constant attribute. With T the one such position it seeks on TAEV with no validation. With V and T both bound it seeks on T for an attribute not declared many (SEEK T6), with no validation.
- **P9** (the join routes). `matchWithHashJoin` and `matchWithMergeJoin` wrap their run in the resolving iterator outside History and admit each emission through `bindingMatchPlan.matches` or `matchesWithBindingTuple`, each a `matchesDatom` call with every position's constant or binding value.
- **P10** (the cache arms). `matchUnboundScan` enters `matchFromCache` when the matcher has a cache, is not in History, and E and A are bound, under a condition that does not read `tx`; the function takes `e`, `a`, `v`, the definition and the value type and no T, and builds each tuple from the entry's value and `entry.Version()`. `matchWithBindingsFromCache` reads E from each binding tuple, V from P's constant or the binding, no T, and builds its tuples from `entry.Version()`.
- **P11** (the entry's version). `rebuildOne` stores in `CacheEntry.version` the ElementID `ResolveLWW` returns. Under the plain rule that is the resolved datom's Tx. Under the unique rule `ResolveLWW` returns the walk's version, "the highest among the datoms it read, other entities' superseding claims included", and discards the emitted datom's own Tx, which `walkUniqueEntityValue` returns as its second result. `rebuildMany` stores the set's highest ElementID. `CacheEntry` has no field for a resolved datom's Tx.
- **P12** (as-of views). Every scan, `ResolveLWW`, the walk and `resolveAVLWW` skip a datom above the target through `shouldFilterTx`, and an as-of matcher reads its view's own cache (`Cache.ForView`).
- **P13** (the fifth position). In a resolving view `MatchWithConstraints` routes every five-element pattern through `matchAsAssertions` before any other route: a constant other than true answers nothing, true and a blank match the four-element pattern, and a variable binds true beside each tuple.
- **P14** (History). In History `matchUnboundScan` takes neither the V-constant route nor the cache arm, every scan is raw, and `matchesDatom` admits every datom carrying the bound positions' values.
- **P15** (the many and vector arms). `matchUnboundScan` sends a pattern on an attribute resolving as many or vector, outside History, to arms of their own, and none of them calls `matchesDatom`. `matchCardinalityManyAsRelation` and `cardinalityManyScanAllEntitiesIterator` bind a T variable to the set's version. `matchCardinalityManyMembership` and `cardinalityManyAVETValueIterator` bind it to the zero ElementID. `matchCardinalityVectorAsRelation` binds it to the group's highest ElementID. None compares a bound T.
- **P16** (`resolveAVLWW`). The function answers the claimant of a unique value and the Tx of the claimant's own assertion, `walkUniqueEntityValue`'s second result.
- **P17** (the cache's cost). A hit in the EA cache is a routed read of one slot, measured at 6.1 ns on the complex checkpoint (the janus `CLAUDE.md`, the EA-cache trie case study), and a miss is one resolution of the pair.

## 3. Theorems

**T1 (what a bound T selects).** In a resolving view, by the definition of the answer and P1, a pattern with T bound answers the resolved datoms whose Tx is T, among those its other bound positions admit. `[?e ?a V T]` answers the pairs whose resolved datom carries V and was written by T. `[?e ?a ?v ?t] [(= ?v V)] [(= ?t T)]` answers the same pairs: its pattern binds no position, so it answers every resolved datom, and the predicates keep the ones carrying V and T. The two spellings are one relation. ∎

**T2 (a whole-group run is the answer).** Under P2, P4 and P5, the unbound route over a run under an E or A prefix emits exactly the view's resolved datoms of the groups in the run, and under P1 admits each by every bound position, T included. Under P9 the join routes over such a run do the same. So `[E A ?v T]`, `[E ?a ?v T]` and `[?e A ?v]` with T free answer T1's relation, and so does every binding whose seek dimension is E or A. ∎

**T3 (a run narrowed to T is not the answer).** Under P6 the run under `[T]` or `[A][T]` holds one operation's datoms, and the iterator emits each cardinality-one assertion in it as its group's first datom. The group's resolved datom is that emission exactly when no datom of the group with a higher Tx is in the view and, under the unique rule, when the walk settles on it. A pair the view holds a later datom of has its resolved datom outside the run, and the emission is a datom the view does not resolve to. Under P2 and P3 the unbound route admits it, since `matchesDatom` compares T against the emission's own Tx, which is T. Under P8 and P9 the T-seek binding route admits it the same way. So `[?e ?a ?v T]`, `[?e A ?v T]`, and `[?e ?a V ?t]` with `?t` bound by an input answer, beside T1's relation, every datom of T whose pair a later operation wrote. INDEX Theorem 1(b)'s "CRDT resolution is trivially correct" holds within the run: the run holds no second datom of the pair, and the datom the view resolves the pair to is the one it leaves out. ∎

**T4 (a run narrowed to V is not the answer for T).** Under INDEX Theorem 2 and P7 the V-constant route's candidate for a cardinality-one pair is the pair's newest datom carrying V, and `validateCandidate` keeps it when the pair's resolved datom carries V. The resolved datom's Tx is never read. Under the plain rule the newest datom carrying V is the resolved datom when the resolved datom carries V, so the kept candidate's Tx is the resolved datom's, and the route's answer for `[?e ?a V T]` is every pair whose resolved datom carries V, whatever T. Under the unique rule `tryEmitUniqueWinner` emits the claimant with the Tx of its own assertion (P16) and reads no T. So `[?e ?a V T]`, `[?e A V T]` and `[?e ?a ?v T]` with `?v` bound by an input answer T1's relation with T struck from it. ∎

**T5 (the cache arm is not the answer for T).** Under P10 the cache arm admits the pair's resolved value whatever T binds, and binds a T variable to the resolution's version. Under P11 the version is the resolved datom's Tx under the plain rule and exceeds it under the unique rule whenever the walk read another entity's later claim or a later assertion of the pair's own it skipped. So `[E A V T]` with the cache on answers E whatever T, where the same pattern with the cache off takes the EATV run and answers E exactly when the resolved datom's Tx is T (T2), and `[E A ?v ?t]` on a unique attribute binds `?t` to a Tx of no datom of the pair with the cache on and to the resolved datom's with it off. The janus `CLAUDE.md`'s rule for cache changes, that a test runs cache on and off and both answer alike, names this divergence. ∎

**T6 (validation against the pair's resolution restores the answer).** Let a candidate be a datom of pair (E, A) from a run narrowed to V or to T, and let d* be the pair's resolved datom in the view, read through the cache entry when the entry carries the resolved datom's Tx and through `ResolveLWW` when it returns that Tx. The pattern admits d* exactly when `matchesDatom(d*, e, a, v, tx, nil)` holds with the bound positions' values (P1). Emitting d*'s tuple when it holds, and nothing when it does not or the pair resolves to no datom, answers T1's relation restricted to the pairs the run holds a datom of. Every pair whose resolved datom carries V has a datom carrying V in the V-run (INDEX Theorem 5), and every pair whose resolved datom was written by T has that datom in the T-run (P6), so the restriction loses nothing. This is INDEX Theorem 5 with the Tx beside the value: one resolution per candidate, each a cache read or an EATV scan of the pair. ∎

**T7 (the T-run is the narrower candidate run).** For `[?e ?a V T]` and `[?e A V T]` both narrowings exist. The run narrowed to T holds the datoms of one operation. The run narrowed to V holds every datom ever written carrying V, across every entity and every operation. Each candidate costs one resolution (T6), so the candidates of the T-run are bounded by the operation's size and those of the V-run by the value's history. With T bound, the candidate run is the T-run, and V is compared on d* (T6). With T free, the candidate run is the V-run as today. ∎

**T8 (the view as of T).** In the view as of T, under P12, no datom above T is read, so a cardinality-one pair's datom written by T is the group's highest in the view, and no other entity's claim in the view is later than it. The T-run's emission is then the pair's resolved datom unless it withdraws (P5), and T6's validation keeps it. So in the view as of T, `[?e ?a ?v T]` answers the assertions T wrote, and in the latest view it answers those among them that still stand: the contrast the tests pin. ∎

**T9 (History is unchanged).** Under P14 a History pattern takes no validating route and no cache arm, and `matchesDatom` admits every datom carrying the bound values. `[?e ?a V T]` in History answers every datom T wrote carrying V, and the resolution restates nothing of that. ∎

**T10 (the fifth position is decided before every route).** Under P13 the fifth position never reaches the routes this proof changes: a false constant answers nothing, and true, a blank and a variable reduce to the four-element pattern. The fifth position is the subject of D3 of the review this proof answers, at the executor's fallback arm, another site. ∎

**T11 (the many and vector arms hold three answers for T).** Under P15 the direct arms bind a many member's T to the set's version or to the zero ElementID and compare no bound T, and under P5 and P9 the join routes bind it to the member's own assertion and compare T against it. Under the definition of the resolved datoms, a member's resolved datom is its newest assertion, so the join routes' answer is the contract's and the direct arms' is not: the set's version is the Tx of some operation on the group, not of the member's assertion, and the zero ElementID is no datom's. For a vector the direct arm, the cache arm and the resolving iterator agree on the group's highest ElementID. ∎

## 4. Lemmas

### L1. The review's run (T4)

Let first and second each hold `:item/one "x"`, declared cardinality one, first's assertion written by T1 and second's by T2, and let the latest view be read. `[?e ?a ?v ?t] [(= ?v "x")] [(= ?t T1)]` answers first: both pairs resolve to their one datom, and the predicates keep first's. `[?e ?a "x" T1]` takes the V-constant route: the VAET run under `"x"` holds first@T1 and second@T2, each its group's one datom, each emitted; `validateCandidate` answers that each pair's current value is `"x"`; both are admitted. The answer is first and second, where T1's relation holds first alone. The same for `[?e :item/one "x" T1]` on AVET, for `[?e ?a ?v T1]` with `?v` bound to `"x"` by an input, and on a named source and in a rule body, which reach the same route. ∎

### L2. The superseded datom under the T-run (T3)

Let first assert `:item/one "x"` by T1 and `"z"` by T3, and let the latest view be read. First's resolved datom is `"z"`@T3. `[?e ?a ?v T1] [(= ?v "x")]` takes the TAEV run under T1, which holds first@T1 `"x"` alone for the pair; the iterator emits it; `matchesDatom` compares T1 with T1 and admits it. The answer is first, where the answer of `[?e ?a ?v ?t] [(= ?v "x")] [(= ?t T1)]` is empty, since first's resolved datom carries `"z"` and T3. In the view as of T1 both answer first (T8). ∎

### L3. The cache arm (T5)

With L1's data and the cache on, `[#id "item:first" :item/one "x" T2]` enters `matchFromCache`: the entry's value is `"x"`, equal to the bound value, and the tuple is built; T2 is never compared with the entry's version T1. The answer is first. With the cache off the EATV run under `[first][:item/one]` resolves to first@T1 and `matchesDatom` compares T2 with T1; the answer is empty, which is T1's relation. ∎

### L4. The version and the walk's datom (T5)

Let holder assert `:item/code "y"` by T1 and `"x"` by T2, and claimant assert `"x"` by T3, `:item/code` declared unique. Holder's resolved datom is `"y"`@T1 (SCHEMA, "Read-time resolution"). `walkUniqueEntityValue` reads holder@T2, opens the supersession scan that reads claimant@T3, skips holder@T2, and emits holder@T1 with the version T3; `ResolveLWW` returns the version; `rebuildOne` stores it. `[#id "item:holder" :item/code ?v ?t]` with the cache on binds `?t` to T3, a Tx of claimant's datom, and with the cache off the EATV run's unique walk emits holder@T1 and binds `?t` to T1. ∎

### L5. The value asserted twice (T6)

Let first assert `:item/one "x"` by T1 and `"x"` again by T4. First's resolved datom is `"x"`@T4. `[?e ?a "x" T1]` under T6 takes the T-run under T1, whose candidate is first@T1; the pair resolves to first@T4; `matchesDatom` compares T1 with T4 and admits nothing. `[?e ?a "x" T4]` admits first. The V-run's candidate is first@T4, the newest datom carrying `"x"`, which resolves to itself, and T6 admits it for T4 and not for T1. The two runs agree, and both agree with `[?e ?a ?v ?t] [(= ?v "x")] [(= ?t T)]`. ∎

### L6. The binding with T (T3, T6)

With L2's data, `[:find ?e :in $ ?t :where [?e ?a "x" ?t]]` given T1 binds T alone, so `analyzeReuseStrategy` seeks on TAEV (P8) and the hash join opens the run under T1, whose emission first@T1 the probe admits, since the binding's T is T1 and the datom's is T1. The answer is first, where T1's relation is empty. Under T6 the run is a candidate run and first@T1 resolves to first@T3, which the bound T does not admit. The same with `?v` and `?t` both bound, where T is the seek dimension (P8). ∎

### L7. The many member's T (T11)

Let e assert `:tags "a"` by T1 and `"b"` by T2, `:tags` declared many. `[#id e :tags ?v ?t]` through `matchCardinalityManyAsRelation` answers `("a", T2)` and `("b", T2)`, the set's version beside each member. A binding-driven `[?e :tags ?v ?t]` with `?e` bound and the cache off takes a join route over EAVT, whose resolving iterator emits `"a"`@T1 and `"b"`@T2, and answers `("a", T1)` and `("b", T2)`. The members' resolved datoms are `"a"`@T1 and `"b"`@T2. ∎

## 5. What the proof finds

- **F1.** The fact is one, which datom of a pair a pattern with T bound admits in a resolving view, and the tree computes it by two rules. The whole-group runs and the join routes compare every bound position against the pair's resolved datom (T2). The V-constant route and the cache arms compare no T (T4, T5), and the T-narrowed runs compare T against a datom that is not the pair's resolved one (T3).
- **F2.** The cache entry carries the resolution's version and no resolved datom's Tx (P11). The version is what the cache's freshness reads, and it is the resolved datom's Tx under the plain rule alone. A reader of the T position through the cache has nothing to read for a unique attribute, and `matchFromCache` binds the version in its place (L4).
- **F3.** `ResolveLWW` holds the resolved datom's Tx in the unique arm and returns the version in its place. The walk returns both (P11).
- **F4.** The review's reference instrument, `[?e ?a ?v T] [(= ?v "x")]`, is the T-run of T3. It answers the contract on data no later operation touched, L1's, and answers a superseded datom on L2's. The reference that holds on every input binds T free and filters it: `[?e ?a ?v ?t] [(= ?v V)] [(= ?t T)]` (T1).
- **F5.** The T position of a many member has three answers in the tree, and one of them is the contract's (T11, L7). The direct arms' answer, the set's version, is INDEX's matrix row for the many cardinality read as a T: the row's `*` says Tx is not consulted, which is true of the run and not of the answer. The sites that compute the version or the zero ElementID as a member's T are `matchCardinalityManyAsRelation`, `cardinalityManyScanAllEntitiesIterator`, `matchCardinalityManyMembership`, `cardinalityManyAVETValueIterator`, and the cache arms' many case, whose entry holds no member's Tx. Each is F1's fact computed by a third rule. The resolution (§7) gives a member's T one computation at every arm, the member's newest assertion's Tx, carried from the set's resolution. The vector direct arm, the cache arm's vector case and the resolving iterator agree on the group's highest ElementID and stand.
- **F6.** INDEX Theorem 1(b) and the selection matrix's TAEV and ATEV rows state the T-narrowed run as CRDT-correct. The statement holds for the run's own groups and for the view as of T (T8), and the latest view resolves a pair the run holds a datom of to a datom outside the run (T3). The rows and the theorem are restated: in a resolving view a T-narrowed run is a candidate run, validated per pair as Theorem 5 validates a V-narrowed one.
- **F7.** Root cause a of the review, a pattern's match decided at sites that read part of it, names the V-constant route and the cache arms. The T-narrowed runs are the same cause from the other side: a site that reads every position of a datom the view does not resolve the pair to.

## 6. The contracts the tests pin

Each contract runs on the legs `legsFor` answers for the test's measured reach, with the EA cache on and off, in the latest view and as of a commit, and each form on `$`, on a named source and in a rule body where the form admits it.

- `[?e ?a V T]`, `[?e A V T]`, `[?e ?a ?v T]` with `?v` from an input, and `[?e ?a V ?t]` with `?t` from an input answer what `[?e ?a ?v ?t] [(= ?v V)] [(= ?t T)]` answers, on L1's data, on L2's, and on L5's (T1, T4, T6, T7).
- `[?e ?a ?v T] [(= ?v V)]` and `[?e A ?v T]` answer the same, on L2's data in the latest view and as of T1 (T3, T8).
- `[?e ?a ?v ?t]` with both bound by a relation input answers the same (T3, T6, L6).
- `[E A V T]`, `[E A ?v T]` and `[E A ?v ?t]` answer alike with the cache on and off, on L3's data and on L4's, and `?t` is the resolved datom's Tx (T2, T5).
- `[E A ?v T]` through a binding of E, with the cache on and off, answers alike (T5, `matchWithBindingsFromCache`).
- In History, `[?e ?a V T]` answers every datom T wrote carrying V, on L2's data and L5's (T9).
- The unique claimant: on L4's data `[?e ?a "y" T1]` answers holder and `[?e ?a "y" T2]` nothing, with the attribute free and bound (T4, P16).
- A many member, on L7's data, with the cache on and off: `[E A ?v ?t]`, `[E A V ?t]`, `[?e A ?v ?t]`, `[?e A V ?t]` and `[?e A ?v ?t]` with `?e` bound by an input bind each member's own assertion's Tx, History's reading of the assertion. `[E A ?v T]` and `[E A V T]` admit the member whose assertion T is and no other member (T11).
- The public API: `TestReviewAConstantValueWithAFreeAttributeThroughThePublicAPI`'s assertion that `[?e ?a "x" #eid [lamport replica]]` answers the assertion the transaction names, through `db.OpenMemory`.

## 7. The resolution

The resolved datom's Tx is a fact of the resolution beside its version, and every reader of the T position reads it.

- `ResolveLWW` returns the resolved datom's Tx and the version as two results. Under the plain rule they are one ElementID. Under the unique rule the Tx is the walk's second result and the version its third. `CacheResolver` declares the signature, `rebuildOne` stores the Tx in a field of `CacheEntry` beside `version`, and `lookupAttribute` reads what it read before.
- The validating iterator carries P's T, the constant or the binding tuple's through `ExtractT`, and compares it as it compares V: `resolvedDatom` answers a cardinality-one pair's resolved datom, its value and its Tx, through the entry or `ResolveLWW`, and `Next` admits the candidate when `matchesDatom` holds on that datom with the bound V and T, emitting the resolved datom's tuple. `tryEmitUniqueWinner` admits the claimant when its assertion's Tx is the bound T. - A many candidate from the T-run resolves through `PatternMatcher.resolvedMemberDatom`, the one reader of a member's own EAVT run under `[E][A][V]`, which answers the newest assertion the add-wins rule keeps as the member's datom and nothing when the member is withdrawn or was never asserted. `matchCardinalityManyMembership` reads the same function over the same run. `CRDTResolvingIterator.processAddWins` answers a member's datom over a whole group, which the T-run is not.
