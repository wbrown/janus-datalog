## 1. Definitions

Every name below is the code's, given a symbol for the proof.

- A **rule** r is the head `(p H)` over the symbols H and the body c_1, …, c_n (`Rule`).
- A **binding pattern** β of p marks each position bound or free. **H_B** is the head symbols at the bound positions (`rewritePredicate`'s `boundVars`).
- An **order** of r's body is a permutation of its clauses. The written order is the body's own. The planner's order is the clauses of the phases `PlanQuery` returns for the body framed as a scope under H_B, less the `scope/required` invocation that opens them (NESTED §5.5, SCOPE §4.1). `rewriteClauses` walks the planner's order.
- For an invocation q in the body, **P(q)** is the clauses before q under the order. _*P*(q)_* is the clauses of P(q) a magic rule may read: a clause that is not a `not` or a `not-join`, every mandatory correlate of which is in bound(q) or extent(q) as they stand at that clause. An `or-default-join` among them is read as the union of its branches over its header, which binds what the form binds and holds every tuple it yields (`magicRuleReads`, `rewriteClauses`).
- A clause of P*(q) is **seed-connected** or an **extent clause**, a position of q is **required**, and a scope's **extent roots** are, as `A_CLAUSE_OUTSIDE_THE_SEED_COMPONENT_BINDS_NOTHING_PROOF.md` §1 defines them. A data pattern with a constant at the entity position, which names one entity, is seed-connected. The **extent chain** of a symbol is that proof's too.
- **bound(q)** is H_B plus every variable of a seed-connected clause of P*(q), and **extent(q)** is the extent roots and every symbol an extent clause of P*(q) provides, less bound(q), which `rewriteClauses` computes as `before.bound` and `before.extent` after each clause it reads.
- The **adornment** γ of q binds a position whose argument is a constant, a variable of bound(q), or a variable of extent(q) at a required position, a variable at its first position (`adornInvocation`).
- The **guard** of r is `(magic.p^β H_B)`.
- **R(q)**, the clauses the magic rule reads, is the seed-connected clauses of P*(q) and the extent chain of every variable of extent(q) that γ binds. The **magic rule** of q is `(magic.q^γ A_γ) :- guard R(q)`. A_γ is the bound arguments, and a constant argument is grounded into a fresh symbol (`adornInvocation` over `in.prefix`).
- The **adorned rule** of r is `(adorned.p^β H) :- guard c'_1 … c'_n`, with each adorned invocation in its clause's place (`rewritePredicate`).
- The **seed** is the top-level invocation's bound values projected onto H_B (`seedRelation`). They are the facts of `magic.p^β` no magic rule derives.
- The **rewritten set** M is the adorned and magic rules of every predicate the bindings reach, built through `NewSeededRuleSet` (`Magic`).
- Two clauses are **adjacent** when they share a variable. The **seed component** of a magic rule's body is the guard and every clause connected to it by adjacency within that body.
- The **find boundary** (SCOPE §3): a magic rule's body runs as a query finding the rule's head (RULES §1.3). Its clauses collapse into groups that share symbols. A group the find reads no symbol of is an existence verdict (`existenceVerdict`). When the find's symbols lie in one group, that group is projected. When they span several groups, the answer is the groups' product (`Execute`).
- A clause **scans a whole extent** when it reads nothing bound under the symbols available when it is selected (`scansAWholeExtent`). For a data pattern, that is no constant at a position other than the attribute and no available variable. For an invocation, that is no constant argument, no available argument, a predicate the seed does not restrict (`SeedRestricted`: a magic or adorned predicate, the delta of one, or `scope/required`), and a predicate that is no delta (`IsDeltaSymbol`), since a delta holds the tuples the iteration before added (`DELTA_LEADS_ITS_COPY_PROOF.md`). For any other clause, that is no available variable among the ones it provides or correlates on: a form whose header nothing binds, a subquery with no available input, or an expression over constants alone. Such a clause reads its whole extent. The pattern reads every datom of its attribute. The invocation reads its predicate whole. A form runs each branch over the unit relation (SCOPE §4.4). A subquery runs its inner query over no input. An expression computes from constants alone. **Visible selectivity** is the opposite: a constant, an available variable, or a seed restriction. A `not-join` has none. It provides nothing, so it links no chain of bindings, and its body runs over its subject's windows whichever unrelated clause runs first, so its place among the clauses without it is its score's.
- The **planner's order** within a phase is `selectPhaseClauses`'s. Among the clauses `clauseSelectable` admits, it takes the clause of the highest rank, and within a rank the clause `scoreClause` scores highest, the first in list order on a tie. A delta invocation has rank 2 (`DELTA_LEADS_ITS_COPY_PROOF.md`), a clause that scans a whole extent has rank 0, and every other clause has rank 1. The clause selected makes the symbols it provides available to the clauses after it. A clause is selectable when every mandatory correlate of it is available and no gate holds it.
- The **gate** is `invocationDependsOnPendingProvider` for an invocation and `subqueryDependsOnPendingProvider` for a subquery. The first holds an invocation of a predicate the seed does not restrict while a selectable clause, other than a subquery or an invocation, provides one of its unavailable variable arguments. The second holds a subquery while a clause other than a subquery provides one of its unavailable binding variables, selectable or waiting on no output of the subquery. Under either, a clause with an available input, an argument or a subquery input, is held by no provider that scans a whole extent (`scansAWholeExtent`).

## 2. Preconditions

- **P1.** The rule set is safe and stratifies (`NewRuleSet`), and so does M. A site whose adornment would not stratify is left unadorned and the set rebuilt without it, so every rewrite is accepted (`NON_MONOTONE_FORMS_PROOF.md` T6, L4).
- **P2.** Each stratum of M evaluates to its fixpoint (SCOPE §4.2).
- **P3.** The relation "q′ stands in P(q)" over the body's invocations is acyclic. An order gives it.

## 3. Theorems

**T1 (correctness under every order).** For every order of every body, `adorned.p^β` holds exactly the tuples of p whose bound positions hold a seed tuple. For every adorned q^γ of M, `adorned.q^γ` holds exactly the tuples of q whose γ-bound positions hold a tuple of `magic.q^γ`.

**T2 (a binding from outside the seed component).** Let c be a clause of P*(q) outside the seed component of q's magic rule. Let c provide a variable x at a position of q, and let q have a position bound from the seed component. Let γ be an adornment of q binding x's position, its magic rule reading c, and γ⁻ the adornment with x's position free. Then:

- (a) The magic rule's find spans the seed component's group and the group holding c, and `magic.q^γ` is their product projected onto A_γ. When c's group is the only other group the find reads, |magic.q^γ| = |magic.q^γ⁻| · |π_x(G_c)|, with G_c the group holding c.
- (b) `adorned.q^γ` is `adorned.q^γ⁻` restricted to the tuples whose x-position lies in π_x(G_c). The adorned rule of p holds c in every order, so p's result is the same under γ and γ⁻.
- (c) Each iteration of q's stratum reads at least |magic.q^γ| keys. Binding x bounds the work by |π_x(G_c)|, the extent of c's relation less what c's own constants leave, a number the engine cannot see (SCOPE §4.6). Under γ⁻ the work is bounded by what the seed reaches.

**T3 (the planner's order under the rank).** In every phase, an invocation one of whose positions the seed component binds is selected before any clause that scans a whole extent, and every clause before it in the order has visible selectivity.

**T4 (the closure).** Take the recursion-first closure `[(follows ?a ?c) (follows ?a ?b) [?b :page/next ?c]]` under `bf`. The planner adorns its invocation `bf`, with the magic rule `(magic.bf/follows ?a) :- (magic.bf/follows ?a)`. Take the binder-after closure `[(follows ?a ?c) [?a :page/next ?b] (follows ?b ?c)]` under `bf`. The planner adorns its invocation `bf`, with the magic rule `(magic.bf/follows ?b) :- (magic.bf/follows ?a) [?a :page/next ?b]`. Both hold on both planner paths, and whichever way each body is written. The written order gives the same two rewrites.

**T5 (two readers of one order).** `BoundPositions`, read by `deriveInvocation` at the clause and by the planner's walk at `PlanQuery`, binds a top-level invocation's position by a constant, the environment, or a relation group in hand, the result of the planned clauses before it. The rewrite binds a position by a constant, a variable of bound(q), or a variable of extent(q) at a required position (§1, `A_CLAUSE_OUTSIDE_THE_SEED_COMPONENT_BINDS_NOTHING_PROOF.md` T2). Both read the planner's order, and T3's rank serves both. Without the rank, the top level would pay T2's product with no rewrite reading an order: with `[:find ?c :in $ % ?a :where (follows ?a ?b) [?b :page/next ?c]]` ordered pattern first, `BoundPositions` binds both positions and `seedRelation` seeds `magic.bb/follows` with every page carrying a next, beside `?a`.

## 4. Lemmas

### L1. Soundness: `adorned.q^γ` ⊆ q for every adorned predicate of M

By induction on the iterations of the fixpoint. At iteration zero every adorned relation is empty. A tuple derived at iteration i by an adorned rule of q^γ is derived by that rule's body. That body is an original rule's body with the guard conjoined and each invocation replaced by its adorned predicate. By the hypothesis each adorned predicate holds a subset of its original, and the guard only removes tuples. So the same original rule derives the tuple in the original set. ∎

### L2. Completeness: every tuple of q whose γ-bound positions hold a tuple of `magic.q^γ` is in `adorned.q^γ`

Let t be a tuple of p whose bound positions hold a seed tuple. Take a derivation of t in the original set of least depth d. The claim is proved by induction on d, and at each depth by induction on the clause position within the body of the rule r that derives t under the substitution σ.

The guard holds for t by the hypothesis. Walk r's body in the order. A clause c_i that is a data pattern, a predicate or an expression holds under σ, as in the derivation. For an invocation q at position i, every clause of P*(q) holds under σ. A pattern holds in the store. An adorned invocation before q holds its tuple σ(q′) by the inner induction. A fallback form is read as the union of its branches over its header, which holds under σ, since the branch the fallback answered σ from holds under σ. A negation is not read (`magicRuleReads`). So the magic rule of q derives σ(A_γ), and σ(A_γ) is in `magic.q^γ`. The tuple σ(q) of q has a derivation of depth below d, so by the outer induction σ(q) is in `adorned.q^γ`. Every clause of r's adorned body holds under σ, and `adorned.p^β` derives t. ∎

P3 enters at the inner induction. The magic rule of q reads adorned invocations before q alone, so their tuples are established before q's magic tuple is needed.

### L3. A cyclic prefix function derives nothing

Take `[(p ?a ?d) (q ?a ?b) (r ?a ?c) [?b :e ?d] [?c :f ?d]]` under `bf`. Let the prefix function give each invocation every other clause connected to H_B through the body, so r stands in P(q) and q in P(r). Then bound(q) holds `?a`, `?c`, `?d` and `?b`, q is adorned `bb`, and its magic rule reads `adorned.bb/r`. Symmetrically r is adorned `bb` and its magic rule reads `adorned.bb/q`. From the seed alone `magic.bb/q` has no tuple until `adorned.bb/r` has one. That waits on `magic.bb/r`, which waits on `adorned.bb/q`, which waits on `magic.bb/q`. The fixpoint leaves all four empty and `adorned.bf/p` empty, where the original set derives p's tuples. So the prefix function is an order (P3), never the set of clauses connected to H_B. ∎

### L4. The product (T2a)

In `Execute`'s find boundary over several groups, a find symbol in no group is an error. A group holding every find symbol is projected and returned. Otherwise the groups' product is projected. The magic rule's find is A_γ. The argument the seed component binds lies in the seed component's group, and x lies in G_c. The two groups are distinct, since c is outside the seed component and the groups are the body's connected components. So the find spans two groups, the product is taken, and its projection onto A_γ is the product of the projections. ∎

### L5. The restriction and the work (T2b, T2c)

(b) By T1, `adorned.q^γ` is q restricted to `magic.q^γ`. By L4 that magic set is `magic.q^γ⁻` crossed with π_x(G_c). So `adorned.q^γ` is `adorned.q^γ⁻` restricted to the tuples whose x-position lies in π_x(G_c). The clause c stands in p's adorned body in every order, since `rewritePredicate` keeps every clause. So a tuple of `adorned.q^γ⁻` whose x-position lies outside π_x(G_c) is dropped at c, and p's result is the same.

(c) Each rule of q^γ opens with the guard `(magic.q^γ A_γ)`. The guard is a seed-restricted invocation, which the planner leads with (`scoreClause`, `SeedRestricted`). So each iteration of the stratum joins the guard's every tuple with the body's first pattern, seeking per key (SCOPE §4.6): at least |magic.q^γ| keys per iteration. ∎

### L6. The rank (T3)

Let q be an invocation one of whose positions the seed component binds. The seed component's path from H_B to that argument is a chain of clauses. Each shares a variable with the one before, so each has visible selectivity once the one before has been selected. Under the rank, a clause that scans a whole extent is selected only when no other clause is selectable. While the chain's next link is selectable, no scan is selected. A link a gate holds waits on a clause the gate names, which is selectable and ranks above a scan: a pattern waits on a pending expression, a subquery on a provider, an invocation on a provider. So the chain completes before any scan is selected. When q's argument is available, q has visible selectivity and is selectable, because the gate holds it only behind a provider with visible selectivity, which is selected before q and before any scan. So q is selected before any scan, and every clause selected before q has visible selectivity. ∎

### L7. The closure (T4)

Take H_B = {`?a`} available. In the recursion-first body, `(follows ?a ?b)` has an available argument. `[?b :page/next ?c]` scans a whole extent, so it ranks below the invocation, and the gate releases the invocation, since a provider that scans a whole extent does not hold an invocation with an available argument. So the invocation is selected first, P*(q) is empty, γ is `bf`, and the magic rule is the guard alone. In the binder-after body, `[?a :page/next ?b]` has an available variable. `(follows ?b ?c)` has no available argument, and the gate holds it behind that pattern. So the pattern is selected first, P*(q) is the pattern, γ is `bf` with `?b` bound, and the magic rule reads the guard and the pattern. Written the other way round, each body gives the same two selections, since the rank and the gate read availability and not list order. On the algebra path the decompiled clauses enter the same selection. `rewriteClauses` in the written order makes the same two choices. ∎

### L8. The top level (T5)

`deriveInvocation` marks a variable argument bound, through `BoundPositions`, when the environment's symbols or the groups' symbols hold it. A query input is in the environment. The groups are the results of the clauses the planner placed before the invocation, in its phase and the phases before it. So the invocation's bound set is the environment plus what the planned clauses before it provide, whatever each shares with the environment. The rewrite's bound set is H_B plus what the seed-connected clauses before it provide (§1), so the top level binds from a clause outside the seed component where the rewrite does not, and the two read one order. For the query of T5, the rank selects the invocation first: `?a` is available and the pattern scans a whole extent. So no group holds `?b` when the invocation is bound, and `seedRelation` builds one seed tuple from `?a`. ∎

## 5. What the proof finds

- **F1.** The bindings pass through an order (L3). A set of clauses connected to the bound head positions, read as the prefix of every invocation, leaves mutually connected invocations each waiting on the other's magic tuples. So the rewrite reads an order and computes none of its own. That is NESTED §5.5's rule and SCOPE §4.1's one orderer.
- **F2.** Correctness holds under every order (T1). What the order decides is which clauses stand before each invocation, and through them the magic set's size (T2). A clause outside the seed component placed before an invocation the seed component binds multiplies the magic set by an extent the engine cannot see where the invocation is bound from it (T5, F5).
- **F3.** The rank removes this failure. Without it, the score placed a scan of a whole extent before an invocation with an available argument, and the gate held the invocation behind that scan. Either alone sufficed (L7). The written order placed the invocation first for the closure, so a rewrite reading the planner's order adorned `bb` where the written order adorned `bf` (T4).
- **F4.** The same placement bound a top-level invocation after a scan of the attribute the scan reads, and seeded the magic set with the product (T5). The defect was the planner's, and the top level paid it with no rewrite reading an order.
- **F5.** The rank and the gate's condition keep every clause outside the seed component that has no visible selectivity behind the invocation (T3). A pattern with a constant beyond its attribute, or an invocation with a constant argument, outside the seed component, may still precede the invocation. That placement is the planner's bet: what the constant leaves of the clause's extent is a number the engine cannot see (SCOPE §4.6). The rewrite binds a position from such a clause only when the clause is a data pattern with an entity constant, which names one entity, or the position is required (§1, `A_CLAUSE_OUTSIDE_THE_SEED_COMPONENT_BINDS_NOTHING_PROOF.md` T2, T3). The top level binds from every group in hand (T5), so a top-level invocation placed after such a clause pays the product there.
- **F6.** Within a rank the score and the list order decide, and the list order on the algebra path is the decompiled order. Two orders that differ there give two adornments, each correct by T1, as NESTED §5.5 states.
- **F7.** A form whose header no available symbol binds runs over the unit relation (SCOPE §4.4). So it ranks with the scans and follows the pattern that binds its header, which is then its subject. A flat `or-join` keeps the seek on the subject's values SCOPE §7 states.

## 6. The rank and the gate

The planner's selection within a phase takes a rank before the score.

- A delta invocation has rank 2, and no gate holds it (`DELTA_LEADS_ITS_COPY_PROOF.md`). A clause that scans a whole extent has rank 0. Every other selectable clause has rank 1. The selection takes the highest rank, and within it the highest score, the first in list order on a tie. Among the clauses of rank 1 the scores and the gates stand as they are.
- `invocationDependsOnPendingProvider` holds an invocation with no available variable argument behind any selectable provider of one of its arguments. It holds an invocation with an available variable argument only behind a provider that does not scan a whole extent.
- `subqueryDependsOnPendingProvider` holds a subquery behind a pending provider of one of its binding variables that is selectable. It also holds the subquery behind a provider that is not selectable but waits, through the pending clauses, on no output of the subquery. It holds a subquery with an available input only behind a provider that does not scan a whole extent, the rule the invocation gate applies to an invocation with an available argument, both reading `scansAWholeExtent`. Under the rank, a subquery reading an available input precedes a scan, so the score no longer places a provider the scan feeds ahead of the subquery, and the gate reaches that provider through `pendingBlockers`, as `patternDependsOnPendingExpression` reaches an expression. A provider waiting on the subquery's own output never holds it, so the phase advances.
